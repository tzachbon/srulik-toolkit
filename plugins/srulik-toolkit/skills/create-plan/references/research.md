# Research planning

Apply these checks to investigations, literature or market research, technical evaluations, experiments, due diligence, and evidence synthesis.

## Frame the research question

Convert the objective into answerable questions and decision relevance:

- primary question
- secondary questions
- decision or action the research will inform
- audience for the result
- evidence threshold required to support or change that decision
- time horizon and freshness requirements
- definition of done

Stop oversized scope early. A research plan should not substitute "research everything about X" for a decision-relevant question.

## Research design

Plan the evidence method before collection:

- source hierarchy and inclusion/exclusion criteria
- search strategy, query progression, or sampling method
- comparison dimensions
- data extraction schema
- treatment of conflicting evidence
- recency and authority requirements
- reproducibility or traceability requirements
- stopping criteria

For empirical work, include hypothesis, variables, controls, sample considerations, analysis method, and stopping criteria as applicable.

## Evidence provenance

Prefer primary evidence when available. When a secondary source cites a consequential statistic, study, policy, standard, or dataset, trace it to the originating source before treating it as established evidence.

Do not count multiple secondary sources that all depend on the same underlying source as independent corroboration.

Record publication date, observation period, or version when freshness changes interpretation.

## Competing explanations

Do not structure research only around confirming the leading hypothesis.

Plan to:

- identify plausible competing explanations
- seek disconfirming evidence
- record what evidence would falsify or materially weaken the preferred conclusion
- explain unresolved contradictions instead of averaging them away

## Bias and uncertainty

Explicitly plan for relevant risks such as:

- confirmation bias
- source selection bias
- survivorship or availability bias
- publication bias
- confounding variables
- missing data
- measurement error
- uncertain generalizability
- evidence dependence on a shared source

Use uncertainty ranges or calibrated confidence language when the evidence does not justify a crisp answer.

## Work items

Use evidence-producing outcomes such as:

1. establish source set and criteria
2. extract comparable evidence
3. verify consequential source claims against primary evidence
4. test competing explanations
5. synthesize findings and uncertainty
6. validate or peer-review the synthesis
7. convert findings into a decision, recommendation, or next experiment

Each work item should state what evidence it produces and how that evidence will be judged sufficient.

## Research QA

Before finalizing verify:

- every research question has a method and evidence source class
- source hierarchy matches the stakes
- freshness requirements are explicit
- contradictions and null results have a treatment plan
- the synthesis cannot silently convert absence of evidence into evidence of absence
- the final decision rule is defined where the research is decision-oriented
